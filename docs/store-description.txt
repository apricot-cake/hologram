=== SUMMARY (max 132 chars) ===

Save posts you find on social media — image, text, and details together — into a searchable library on your PC. Private by design.

=== DETAILED DESCRIPTION ===

Your own personal social-media library.

Corpus saves the posts you come across — not just a screenshot, but the text, author, date, engagement counts, and original URL too — into a library on your own PC, where you can search, tag, and organize them. No more "where did that post go?"

YOUR DATA STAYS WITH YOU
- Everything is saved to a local folder you choose. No data is collected or sent to any server of ours.
- Images and metadata sit side by side as ordinary files. Open them in other tools, back them up, move the whole library — it is all yours.
- Your library stays with you even when a post or account is no longer available online.
- Export/import the whole library as a ZIP, or set up a scheduled mirror backup to another folder.

HOW TO SAVE
1. Press Alt+S (or click the extension icon), then click a post — it is captured together with its text, author, date, and engagement details.
2. Or drag an image to save an illustration as the image file itself.
3. New captures appear in the desktop app automatically.

FIND IT AGAIN
- Search by text or username, with optional fuzzy matching
- Filter by platform, author, tag, in-text hashtag, date, engagement, and folder — combined freely
- Sort by newest, most likes, save date, and more
- Browse as cards, tiles, or a reading-friendly list; open a gallery that bundles the screenshot and the original-resolution images

ORGANIZE
- Tags, plus a "tagging session" mode to tag untagged posts one after another
- Folders for themes and favorites
- Bulk actions: select many posts and tag, file, group, or delete them at once

SUPPORTED PLATFORMS
X (Twitter) / Bluesky / Misskey / Mastodon / pixiv

REQUIREMENTS
Saving and browsing require the Corpus desktop app (Windows, free and open source). Install it first, then add this extension.

PRIVACY
No data is collected or transmitted to any server of ours. To enrich metadata, read-only requests go directly from your browser to the platform you are viewing (X's syndication API, Bluesky, Misskey, Mastodon, and pixiv — pixiv requests use your own pixiv session so logged-in-only works can be captured; see the privacy policy for details). Everything is saved locally to a folder you choose.
